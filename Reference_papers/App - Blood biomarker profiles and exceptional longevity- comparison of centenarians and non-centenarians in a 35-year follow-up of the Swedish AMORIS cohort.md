\# Blood biomarker profiles and exceptional longevity: comparison of centenarians and non-centenarians in a 35-year follow-up of the Swedish AMORIS cohort

\#\# Biomarker Utility & Assay Technical Validation  
\*   \*\*Marker Classification:\*\* The evaluated biomarkers (metabolic, inflammatory, liver, renal, anemia, and nutritional) are utilized as \*\*prognostic markers\*\* for exceptional longevity (reaching age 100\) \[Introduction, Para 7\]. The study does not evaluate these as predictive markers, as no treatment-by-marker interaction was tested within a randomized framework \[Methods, Para 14\].  
\*   \*\*Assay Technical Validation:\*\* Laboratory analyses were conducted using fully automated procedures on fresh blood samples at the Central Automation Laboratories \[Methods, Para 6\]. The methodology is described as consistent and well-documented, though specific Limit of Detection (LOD) values for the 12 biomarkers are not detailed in the primary text \[Methods, Para 6\].  
\*   \*\*Pre-analytical Specimen Handling:\*\* Analyses were performed on fresh blood samples collected between 1985 and 1996 \[Methods, Para 6\].  
\*   \*\*Diagnostic Accuracy Metrics:\*\* Standard diagnostic metrics (Sensitivity, Specificity, PPV, NPV) were not reported. The study focused on association metrics (Odds Ratios) and distribution quantiles rather than binary classification performance \[Methods, Para 13-14\].  
\*   \*\*Cut-point Selection Analysis:\*\* The study utilized \*\*pre-fixed quintiles\*\* (Q1–Q5) based on the distribution of the entire cohort to categorize continuous biomarker data \[Methods, Para 14\]. This approach mitigates the risk of optimism bias associated with data-derived "optimal" cut-points \[R\_REMARK\_OPTIMIZATION\].

\#\# Clinical Outcome Association & Correlatives  
\*   \*\*Primary Outcome:\*\* The likelihood of reaching the 100th birthday (centenarian status) \[Methods, Para 7\].  
\*   \*\*Statistical Outcomes:\*\*  
   \*   \*\*Uric Acid:\*\* Demonstrated a dose-response relationship; individuals in the lowest quintile (\<310 µmol/L) had nearly double the odds of reaching age 100 compared to the highest quintile (\>430 µmol/L) \[Results, Fig 3\], \[Discussion, Para 68\].  
   \*   \*\*Total Cholesterol (TC):\*\* Higher levels were associated with increased odds of longevity (Q5 vs Q3 OR: 1.06, 95% CI: 0.88–1.27; Q1 vs Q3 OR: 0.67, 95% CI: 0.53–0.85), contradicting typical clinical guidelines for younger populations \[Results, Fig 3\], \[Discussion, Para 73\].  
   \*   \*\*Glucose and Creatinine:\*\* Lower levels were significantly associated with becoming a centenarian (Glucose Q1 vs Q3 OR: 1.20, 95% CI: 0.99–1.45; Creatinine Q1 vs Q3 OR: 1.10, 95% CI: 0.91–1.34) \[Results, Fig 3\].  
   \*   \*\*Liver Function Markers:\*\* Lower levels of ASAT, GGT, ALP, and LD were associated with higher odds of reaching age 100 \[Results, Fig 3\].  
\*   \*\*Centenarian Homogeneity:\*\* Cluster analysis identified two subgroups ("higher nutrition" and "lower but enough nutrition"), yet centenarians generally displayed more favorable and homogenous biomarker profiles starting from age 65 compared to non-centenarians \[Results, Para 20\], \[Discussion, Para 1\].

\#\# Technical Limits & Methodological Assessment  
\*   \*\*Independent Validation:\*\* The study \*\*lacks an independent validation cohort\*\*. While it utilizes a large population-based sample (n=44,636), the findings are derived from a single cohort (AMORIS) without external verification in a separate geographic or ethnic population \[Methods, Para 2, 7\].  
\*   \*\*Verification Bias (R\_STARD\_ACCURACY):\*\* The reference standard (age 100\) was verified via the Swedish Total Population Registry, ensuring objective outcome ascertainment for all participants \[Methods, Para 6\]. Partial verification bias is minimal as follow-up was near-complete, excluding only 0.55% of the cohort due to emigration \[Methods, Para 7\].  
\*   \*\*Confounding and Adjustments:\*\* Models were adjusted for age at measurement, sex, and the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) \[Methods, Para 14\]. Sensitivity analyses adjusting for specific morbidities did not significantly alter the observed associations \[Results, Para 19\].  
\*   \*\*Missing Data:\*\* Multiple imputation was employed to address missing biomarker values, with sensitivity analyses showing more pronounced associations in complete-case data \[Methods, Para 11\], \[Results, Para 19\].  
\*   \*\*Limitations:\*\* The study lacked data on lifestyle factors (smoking, alcohol) and specific inflammatory markers like IL-6 or TNF-alpha, which may confound the relationship between the measured biomarkers and longevity \[Discussion, Para 74\].
